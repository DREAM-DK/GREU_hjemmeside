# Design philosophy

A page has a fixed budget. Not of space, which is free and runs downwards for
ever, but of attention. Every element on the page spends from that budget. The
only question worth asking of any element is whether what it gives back is
worth what it took.

Most decoration gives back nothing. A photograph that could sit on any other
organisation's page says nothing about this one. A colour band behind a
paragraph adds no meaning to the paragraph. Neither is neutral. Both spend
attention the reader brought with them for a different purpose, and readers
have learned to skip anything that looks like decoration. That habit is not
selective. Decoration teaches the eye to skip, and the things standing next to
it get skipped too.

So the rule is simple to state. **Every graphic element must either carry
information or be removed.** Not "earn its place", which is vague enough that
anything can be argued into staying. Carry information. The test is
operational: take the element away and read the page again. If nothing is
lost, nothing was there.

Applying this is harder than the alternative, which is the point of **"do the
hard work to make it simple"**. Adding is easy. It looks like effort, it fills
a brief, and nobody in a meeting objects to more. Removing requires knowing
exactly what the page is for and who is reading it, and then defending an
empty-looking page against people who expect to see work. The simplicity is
the output of that argument, not a way of avoiding it. **"Do less"** is not an
instruction to work less. It is an instruction to decide.

There is a practical dividend. What you do not add cannot break. Every extra
background colour is another set of foreground pairs somebody must check for
contrast, another thing to get wrong when a colour is reused somewhere nobody
tested, another file to serve on every visit. Restraint is cheaper to build,
cheaper to run and cheaper to keep correct.

And it produces distinctiveness rather than costing it. Generic decoration is
by definition available to everyone; a stock photograph of a wind farm belongs
to no one. A graphic that carries information can only be yours, because the
information is yours. Discipline here is not a plainer site. It is a site that
could not be anyone else's.

Finally, and for this project above all: authority comes from restraint. We
are publishing an instrument that ministries will use to score policies,
including policies that score badly. A page that argues looks less trustworthy
than a page that reports. The quieter the presentation, the more credible the
thing presented.
