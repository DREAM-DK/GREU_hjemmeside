## Headline and lead

> Viet Nam: promoting green reforms in a rapidly industrialising country

> From 2026 to 2027 the University of Copenhagen works with three Vietnamese
> policy institutes on tools to assess the energy transition. The project
> first builds a macroeconomic model to assess the macroeconomic and financial
> consequences of different economic growth targets for Viet Nam. It then
> builds a Vietnamese version of GreenREFORM-EU, called GreenREFORM-VN, which
> includes a distributional module. Finally it delivers modelling training
> programmes for the partner institutions and contributes to a series of policy
> studies. The Danish Ministry of Foreign Affairs funds it, and DREAM
> collaborates on the modelling.

## Why it was set up

Viet Nam has grown fast for decades. It has pledged to reach net zero
greenhouse gas emissions by 2050. Energy is an input in every sector, so a
move away from fossil fuels, or a change in the fossil fuel input mix, changes
relative prices, output and incomes across the economy. Those effects have not
yet been quantified. The project sets out to provide a modelling framework
that can quantify them and assist effective climate policy design, by
developing a Vietnamese version of GreenREFORM-EU, called GreenREFORM-VN,
which includes a distributional module.

The project builds on an earlier project funded by the United Nations
Development Programme, which developed an energy social accounting matrix for
Viet Nam for 2019 and a static CGE model for economic analysis of the energy
transition. The present project updates that matrix and uses the data to
calibrate GreenREFORM-VN.

## What it is

The project is separate from the Technical Support Instrument project and from
Version 2.0. It has its own funding and its own team. The University of
Copenhagen leads it, and DREAM collaborates on the modelling.

*Source: approved text. "Collaborates" is the partner's own correction of
"supports"; see What the approval settled.*

## What it sets out to do

1. Bring Danish modelling expertise together, so that Viet Nam can check that
   its growth targets are financially feasible, and can assess the climate and
   social effects of its energy and climate policies.
2. Use the model to assess green reforms and development paths, and to support
   evidence-based policy.
3. Train staff in green CGE modelling at three Vietnamese policy institutes.

*Source: approved text.*

## What the project does

- **Macro model and data.** Update the macroeconomic data and a macro model
  that tests whether growth targets for the next five-year national plan are
  financially feasible.
- **A 2024 data set.** Update the 2019 energy social accounting matrix to 2024.
  The matrix is the data base that the dynamic model needs.
- **A Viet Nam version of the model.** Adapt GreenREFORM-EU to the Vietnamese
  economy and context, as GreenREFORM-VN.
- **Policy questions.** How carbon trading affects the Vietnamese economy, how
  subsidies to green energy production affect dependence on fossil fuel
  imports, and what part energy efficiency can play in the green transition,
  alongside the distributional implications of green reforms.
- **Training and dialogue.** Four workshops: data, macroeconomic modelling,
  the green general equilibrium framework, and applications. Policy events and
  a final conference in Viet Nam.

*Source: approved text.*

## Timeline

- **18 March 2026.** Launch in Hanoi, at the National Institute for Economics
  and Finance.
- **2026 and 2027.** Modelling, data work, workshops and policy studies.
- **December 2027.** The project ends.

A steering committee with the Embassy meets every six months, and a committee
of senior Vietnamese experts provides advice and feedback.

## What the approval settled

The University of Copenhagen returned the approval document on 5 November 2026
with answers written into it. The page was rewritten to match. The substantive
changes, and why each matters:

- **The model has a name: GreenREFORM-VN.** The earlier draft deliberately used
  no name, because the project document and the Embassy said "the GreenReform
  model" while the mid-year report said the Vietnamese partners preferred
  "GreenTransition". UCPH settled it. The site can now use GreenREFORM-VN, and
  it fits the convention already set by GreenREFORM-BE on the Belgium page.
- **DREAM collaborates; it does not support.** The answer was explicit: "there
  is collaboration – but there is no formal support". Every occurrence changed,
  including the Record label, which is now "Modelling collaboration". This is a
  claim about DREAM's own role, so it is the one place where getting it wrong
  would have been DREAM overstating itself.
- **"Static macro model" was rejected as a label.** It is now just the macro
  model. The dynamic model keeps its name. The first model is described by what
  it does — testing whether growth targets are financially feasible — rather
  than by a contrast with the second.
- **Partners are ranked, not listed flat.** NIEF is the main partner and holds
  the contracts with the other two, which are contributing partners. The old
  page implied three equal institutes.
- **The deliverables lost their numbers.** "Six policy briefs", "four policy
  studies", "thirty researchers trained, ten at each institute" all came from
  the project document's results framework. UCPH replaced them with "policy
  briefs and training workshops". Do not reinstate the figures from the project
  document: the partner has chosen not to publish targets it may miss.
- **Coal is out of the framing.** The draft led on phasing out coal power by the
  2040s. The approved text leads on net zero by 2050 and on the fossil fuel
  input mix. Also removed: "The government does not yet have the statistical
  tools to do it", which read as a judgement on the partner's capability.
- **A distributional module is named** for the first time, in the lead and in
  "Why it was set up".
- **An advisory committee of senior Vietnamese experts** was added alongside
  the Embassy steering committee.

Answered and now closed: clearance to name the three institutes and Professor
Tarp, the dates (1 January 2026 to 31 December 2027), naming the Ministry of
Foreign Affairs and the Embassy, and the model name.

## Flags still open

1. **An extension is hoped for, not agreed.** UCPH: "HOPE FOR AN EXTENSION BUT
   NOTHING FORMALLY AGREED AS YET". The page says the project ends in December
   2027, which is right today. Revisit if an extension is signed.
2. **"First progress report" has no address.** UCPH added it to Read more, but
   the line merged with the aid database entry in the returned document and no
   link came with it. It is not on the page. Ask UCPH for the URL, or drop it.
3. **A typo in the approved text was not copied through.** Under "What the
   project does", the approved document reads "Adapt the GreenREFORM-EU model
   to the Vietnamese economy and context, referred to as the GreenREFORM-EU
   model.." — the second name should plainly be GreenREFORM-VN, as it is
   everywhere else in the same document. The page says GreenREFORM-VN. Worth
   mentioning to UCPH so their own copy is corrected.
4. **Money stays off.** DKK 4.8 million in the project document, disbursements
   in the aid database, and DREAM's co-funding of a PhD year, which is not
   public. None is on the page, as on the other project pages.
5. **Julia stays off.** The macro model training uses Julia. That belongs to the
   University's macro model, and naming it here would read as the TSI 2026 code
   conversion. Deliberately omitted, as agreed on 5 November 2026.
6. **Not a TSI or 2.0 output.** Unchanged and still true: "What it is" says so
   in one sentence, and no section lists Viet Nam as a comparison.
7. **Embassy LinkedIn post.** It credits "the DREAM economic model" and the page
   does not link to it. Leave it that way unless the Embassy asks.
