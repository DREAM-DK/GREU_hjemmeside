## Why it was set up

Climate change and climate policy affect the whole economy. Finnish ministries
can assess single measures well. They lack a tool that shows the knock-on
effects across sectors, and the effects on public finances and emissions.

The Ministry of Finance, the government working group on long-term growth
(Room for Growth) and the National Audit Office of Finland all pointed to this
gap. A Ministry of Finance working group reported in June 2026 that Finland
cannot yet assess the economy-wide effects of climate change and the green
transition well enough. It asked for better tools, more skills and lasting
funding.

*Source: TAH Foundation project page and article of 24 September 2026; working
group final report, 24 June 2026.*

## What it is

FinReform is a Finnish national project with its own funding. The model it
adapts is GreenREFORM-EU. Finland's Ministry of Finance took part in the
[TSI 2024 project](project-tsi-2024.md) and in Version 2.0. FinReform is a
separate project. DREAM has no formal role in it.

The project is a pilot. It is meant to show what it takes to use
GreenREFORM-EU in Finland, and which resources keep the analysis going.

*Source: TAH Foundation project page; LinkedIn post by a Ministry of Finance
expert, September 2026.*

## What it sets out to do

- Build a modelling tool that assesses the economic effects of climate change
  and of the policy instruments used against it.
- Adapt GreenREFORM-EU to Finland and put it to use there.
- Make it possible to analyse, in one consistent frame, the measures,
  instruments and choices that the green transition needs.
- Show how climate actions and policy decisions affect the economy, emissions
  and the progress of the transition.
- Build joint learning between research institutes and ministries.

*Source: TAH Foundation project page.*

## What the model covers

The Finnish model covers the whole economy: firms, households, the public
sector, and imports and exports. It includes technologies that cut emissions,
and the accounts for emissions. It can therefore estimate the economy-wide
effects of measures such as technological change in the steel industry or the
electrification of transport.

*Source: TAH Foundation article, 24 September 2026.*

## What the approval settled

Luke returned the document on 4 November 2026 with tracked changes and two
comments. The page was updated to match.

**The ministries were the substantive correction, and it was a correction of
kind, not of number.** The draft called them partners and counted six. Jussi
Lintunen's comments set out three tiers:

> Project partners are Luke, Syke and VTT. Collaborators are ministry of
> finance (VM) and prime minister's office (VNK). Other ministries are involved
> as stakeholders. I would not list them.

> Not as partners but in varying levels of collaboration or in stakeholder
> roles. See the earlier comment.

So the draft was wrong twice over: the ministries are not partners at any tier,
and the flat group of six conflates two collaborating ministries with the rest
as stakeholders. The count was dropped as well as the names. "Six" came from
the TAH Foundation article, whose only framing is the partner one Luke has just
corrected, so keeping the figure in a softer role would have preserved the error
quietly. The page now says the partners are Luke, Syke and VTT, and that Finnish
ministries take part at varying levels of collaboration and as stakeholders.

**"Why it was set up" was deliberately left alone**, even though it names the
Ministry of Finance, the Room for Growth working group and the National Audit
Office. That paragraph cites a published government report of June 2026. Naming
an institution as a *participant* needs its consent; citing its published report
is public record, and the paragraph is the page's strongest evidence for why the
project exists. Luke edited the document freely and did not touch it.

Other changes Luke made:

- **The model examples.** "Forest protection or the electrification of industry"
  became "technological change in the steel industry or the electrification of
  transport", to match the current work.
- **The first example calculations** moved from "around the turn of 2026 and
  2027" to early in 2027.
- **The partner list in the Record** lost "and six ministries".
- **The Finnish title is not needed**, and FinReform is the right spelling. Both
  were open questions; both are now closed.
- **Luke, Syke and VTT all took part in TSI project activities**, not only the
  Ministry of Finance. Luke volunteered this when confirming the earlier-work
  sentence, but did not change that sentence in the page text, so the page still
  credits only the Ministry of Finance. See flag 2.
- **Funding may be acknowledged**, with no set wording required.

## Flags still open

1. **Two named ministries may be publishable.** Jussi named VM and VNK as
   collaborators, but the typed answer says "we would not name the ministries".
   His "I would not list them" most naturally refers to the other ministries,
   the stakeholders, which would mean VM and VNK could be named. The two
   readings differ on whether two ministries appear on a public page, so the
   page names none until Luke confirms. One question settles it.
2. **The earlier-work sentence could be broadened.** The page says Finland's
   Ministry of Finance took part in TSI 2024 and Version 2.0. Jussi added that
   Luke, Syke and VTT were in TSI project activities too. That is not in the
   approved page text, so it is not on the page. Worth asking whether they want
   it, because it would explain why these three institutes are the ones
   building the Finnish model.
3. **"DREAM's role: None" is not in the approved Record.** The approved Record
   has six rows and that is not among them. It was in our earlier draft, and I
   have kept it, because without it a reader on DREAM's own website will assume
   DREAM built the Finnish model. It disclaims rather than claims, and it is a
   statement about DREAM rather than about Luke. Flagging it because it is an
   addition to text a partner has approved, which is not a thing to do silently.
4. **No link and no public contact.** "Project does not yet have pages under
   Luke's domain. Contact Jussi when needed." The page therefore links the TAH
   Foundation and the working group report, not the project. Revisit when Luke
   publishes a page.
5. **Money stays off.** The grant is EUR 280,000 and the TAH Foundation
   publishes it. Not on the page, as on the other project pages.
6. **"Lightened version".** The TAH article calls the TSI 2024 model a lightened
   version of GreenREFORM. The site does not use that wording.
7. **Ministry of Finance pilots.** DREAM's internal March 2026 deck says Finland
   planned pilots on environmental taxation and transport fuels. That deck is
   not public and the page does not use it. Note that Luke's own correction to
   the model examples points elsewhere, to steel and transport electrification,
   which is a reason to keep the deck's version off the page.
8. **Quotes.** The TAH article has a Luke researcher saying the Danish model has
   about 30 full-time staff and that a Finnish equivalent would need sub-models
   and lasting funding. Left out: it is a personal view and might read as a
   comment on DREAM.
