## Why it was set up

Industry accounted for about 46 per cent of Belgian final energy and
non-energy demand in 2019. How much industry Belgium will still host in 2050 is
one of the largest uncertainties in long-term energy planning.

Most net-zero studies treat industrial activity as an assumption. Energy system
models let firms choose between technologies. They do not let firms change what
they produce, where they source inputs, or how much labour they use. A
reduction or increase in industrial demand then enters as an exogenous
assumption. The project asks what happens to the economy and the energy system
when that change has an economic cause whose effect further propagates through
the energy-economy system.

*Source: D2.1, introduction and Chapter 1.*

## What it sets out to do

The project links three kinds of model that are usually run apart: an economic
model, an energy system model and an input-output model of international
supply chains. It works in three steps.

1. **Year one.** Describe industrial transformation cases and group them into
   three storylines: GREEN-IT (core products stay, processes change), SHIFT-IT
   (high-value activity stays, the rest relocates) and LEAVE-IT (energy-intensive
   activity moves out of the EU). Published as Deliverable D1.1.
2. **Year two.** Turn the cases into stylised shocks in the modelling system and
   show what they do. Published as Deliverable D2.1.
3. **Year three.** Combine the shocks into fuller energy-economy pathways, and
   test policy against them.

The work runs in two streams.

- **Stream 1: growth or displacement?** How do large economic trends change
  Belgian manufacturing, and what does that mean for the energy system?
- **Stream 2: to tax or to subsidise?** What happens to emissions and to
  industrial activity under a carbon price, a subsidy for clean technology, or
  both?

*Source: D2.1, Chapter 1.*

## How the models fit together

**Stream 1.** GreenREFORM-BE runs a scenario to 2050. It passes its energy
service demand, by sector and year, to TIB3R, which finds the cheapest
technologies to meet it. The economic model also passes its input-output
tables to an input-output model built on Eurostat's FIGARO tables. That model
traces the environmental impact of Belgian consumption at home and abroad.

**Stream 2.** The energy technology model of GreenREFORM-EU is built into
GreenREFORM-BE. Industries then pick from a list of dated, discrete
technologies, instead of a smooth trade-off between capital and energy. The two
models are solved together, in one system. The technology lists come from TIB3R,
through a Python script written for the project. Steel is the first sector in
the integrated model.

The Belgian version uses fourteen industries. It keeps the elasticities of
GreenREFORM-EU.

*Source: D2.1, Chapter 2.*

## What the October 2026 report shows

These are first results from stylised scenarios. The report says they are not
forecasts and not policy advice. They test what the modelling system can do.

- **The cause of a loss matters, not only its size.** Two pairs of scenarios
  remove the same amount of production from energy-intensive industry. In each
  pair, one version carries a gain in productivity or income and the other does
  not. Energy demand falls by more in the version without a gain. In the trade
  pair, final energy demand falls by 4.1 per cent in one case and 6.7 per cent
  in the other. GDP ends 5.1 per cent above baseline in the first and 0.5 per
  cent below in the second.
- **Electricity shows the widest gap.** In the trade pair, electricity demand
  falls by 5.8 TWh against 13.3 TWh by 2050. The report notes this matters for
  grid planning.
- **Less industry at home is not always less impact.** In both trade scenarios,
  lower impact in Belgium is outweighed by higher impact elsewhere. The global
  environmental footprint may rise.
- **A productivity boom in services can raise industrial energy use.** In one
  scenario on artificial intelligence, higher wages push manufacturing towards
  capital and energy. Final energy demand rises by 5.5 per cent.
- **Technology choice changes the shape of the response.** In the steel test,
  emissions fall in steps tied to the dates when plants retire and new
  technologies become available. A higher carbon price and a clean-technology
  subsidy both bring investment forward. By 2050 the subsidy cuts steel
  emissions by 36 per cent against the baseline. Combined, the two policies do
  more than the sum of their parts until the late 2030s, and less afterwards.

*Source: D2.1, executive summary, Chapters 3 and 4.*

## What comes next

In its final year the project will develop its final energy-economy pathways
and policy analysis. This will involve refining the models, widening the
spectrum of stylised shocks and combining them into coherent scenarios. In
Stream 2 it will add basic chemicals, cement and paper to the integrated model,
and test existing subsidy schemes.

*Source: D2.1, Chapter 5.*

## Relation to GreenREFORM-EU

GreenREFORM-BE is the Belgian implementation of the GreenREFORM-EU model. The
Federal Planning Bureau received the model in the
[TSI 2024 project](project-tsi-2024.md) and has been one of the four teams in
[Version 2.0](project-version-2-0.md). CEEP-IT is a separate Belgian project
with its own funding.

*Source: D2.1, section 2.1.2; project pages.*

## What the approval settled

VITO/EnergyVille and FPB returned the document on 9 November 2026 with 25
tracked changes and two comments. All were accepted. The page was updated to
match.

- **Dates.** October 2024 to September 2027. This replaces the inferred
  "2025 to 2027" in the Record and on the Applications card.
- **Links.** The EnergyVille page is the main project page; the VITO page is
  secondary. Both buttons now point there. The D1.1 PDF link was taken out of
  the buttons and Read more, because its download link is down and "will be
  re-established soon".
- **Lead.** The energy system model is "for Belgium", and the lead now names
  the global input-output model too.
- **Why it was set up.** Industrial demand can rise as well as fall, and enters
  as an exogenous assumption rather than a parameter change. The economic cause
  "further propagates through the energy-economy system".
- **One-way link.** The sentence "The link runs one way: nothing passes back to
  the economic model" was deleted. VITO: "maybe it is not needed to explicitly
  mention we have no feedback to GreenREFORM".
- **Footprint.** "The global environmental footprint rises" became "may rise".
- **Steel numbers (FPB).** The subsidy cuts steel emissions by 36 per cent, not
  30. The combined-policy sentence was rewritten: the two policies do more than
  the sum of their parts until the late 2030s, and less afterwards.
- **What comes next.** Rewritten by VITO. One edit of ours on top: the tracked
  text read "refining the models, widen the spectrum … and combine them"; the
  page makes the three verbs parallel ("refining … widening … combining").
  "Real subsidy schemes" became "existing subsidy schemes".
- **Funder.** "Energy Transition Fund / FPS Economy" in Who takes part. No set
  acknowledgement wording is required beyond the Record.
- **Names and roles.** VITO/EnergyVille coordinates; all three institutions may
  be named.
