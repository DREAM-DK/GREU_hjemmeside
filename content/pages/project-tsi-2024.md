## Why it was set up

The Commission asks Member States to assess the macro-fiscal effects of climate
policy. Real climate plans contain more than a carbon price. They contain
subsidies, mandates and rules tied to particular technologies, such as electric
vehicles, heat pumps and carbon capture. Models used by national teams often
cannot score those policies for emissions and for the economy together.

The Belgian Federal Planning Bureau looked for a model that could. It found the
Danish GreenREFORM. Other institutions joined the request.

*Source: final paper, introduction; Chapter 1 § Background
(`sec:intro-background`).*

## What it set out to do

- Build a customisable "workhorse" version of the Danish GreenREFORM model.
- Give each beneficiary the means to assess the macroeconomic effects of green
  policies and investments.
- Build national modelling capacity on a common framework, so that teams do not
  start from nothing.

The Commission's title for the work is "The GreenREFORM EU model". Chapter 1
gives the formal project title as *Strengthening capacity to model the
macroeconomic effects of 'green' policies and investments through the
GreenREFORM model*.

*Source: Commission project page; Chapter 1 § Background.*

## What it delivered

- **The model.** A dynamic general equilibrium model with forward-looking
  agents and a modular design. Its energy technology model is hard-linked to
  the macroeconomic core. The project papers call that block the abatement
  model.
- **Training.** Two multi-day study visits in Copenhagen, technical workshops
  and methodological training.
- **Data support.** Remote help for teams building their own national data
  sets.
- **A benchmark data set.** Produced by Statistics Denmark. It has 57
  industries, 25 energy products and five emission types. It shows how the
  model runs and what a country data set needs to contain.
- **The final paper.** One carbon tax, simulated twice: with and without the
  energy technology model.

*Source: Commission project page; final paper; DREAM news item, 25 February
2026.*

## Who took part

Five institutions took part:

- Austria: Federal Ministry of Finance
- Belgium: Federal Planning Bureau
- Finland: Ministry of Finance
- Italy: Ministry of Economy and Finance, Department of Finance
- Poland: National Centre for Emissions Management

Four of them worked towards a national version of the model. Poland took part
in the capacity-building activities and did not build a national version.

Country progress is described on the country pages as each institution agrees
to publish it. This page does not report it.

*Source: Commission project page; final paper, acknowledgements and
beneficiary summaries; DREAM news item.*

## Results

The final paper is the project's public result. It is listed under
[Analyses](../analyses/greenreform-eu-model.md). It reports one policy: a
carbon tax of EUR 100 per tonne of CO₂e, phased in to 2030, with the revenue
returned to households. The tax is simulated with and without the energy
technology model.

The paper uses the Danish benchmark data set. It is not a forecast for any
beneficiary country.

*Source: final paper, abstract.*

## Flags for the team

1. **Poland.** The Commission page and the paper list five beneficiaries. DREAM's
   news item says four countries received the model. The paper says Poland did
   not pursue a national version because it uses another model. The draft
   states the fact without the reason. DREAM should agree the wording.
2. **Country progress.** The paper has a paragraph per country. The draft leaves
   them out, because country notes stay off the site until each institution
   agrees. The Belgian paragraph mentions TIMES. Keep that off the core-model
   pages.
3. **Closing seminar.** DREAM's news item says a three-day seminar in Copenhagen
   closed the project "in late September". It gives no year. The original
   project period ended in 2025, so this is September 2025. The draft does not
   use the seminar. Add it if DREAM confirms.
4. **"Simplified".** DREAM's news item calls the model a simplified version of
   the Danish GreenREFORM. The site does not use that wording. Check whether
   DREAM wants it.
5. **Rights and access.** The draft says nothing about licence or access. DREAM's
   abstract says the code is "available on request for advanced users". That
   wording belongs with the Get the model page, once DREAM confirms.
6. **Later projects.** The page no longer separates TSI 2024 from Version 2.0
   and the 2026 TSI project. The text uses only 2024 and 2025 dates, "version
   1.0" and "five institutions", so nothing in it reads as the later projects.
   The Applications page carries the three project cards side by side. The
   Italian Department of Finance cites a Commission decision of 6 March 2026 and
   18 Member States for the 2026 project. Confirm what DREAM wants public.
7. **Commission page documents.** The page ends with a list of related documents.
   My fetch cut it off. Check it for further links.
8. **The paper's date.** The Applications page shows 25 February 2026. That is the
   DREAM publication date. The paper itself says 19 February 2026.
9. **Linking.** The DREAM page and the PDF sit in the Danish catalogue. The
   applications note already suggests republishing the paper on this site.
