## Agents

Households choose how much to consume and save, and how to compose the
consumption bundle. They do not choose labour supply, a portfolio mix, or a
household housing stock. The dwelling stock is building capital of the
housing-services industry.

Firms choose inputs. Some of those choices are static; capital accumulation is
forward-looking. Technology is nested CES, with CET where a firm produces more
than one output. In the current version firms set prices as a mark-up over
unit cost.

Government is an accounting framework, not an optimiser. There is no public
objective function. Revenue is a rate or share on a base determined elsewhere.
Expenditure is a rate or an exogenous path. The block returns a consistent
public budget, public demand on the input-output system, and a closure that
stops the government’s net financial position from drifting.

The rest of the world is a small-open-economy residual: exogenous world
import prices and downward-sloping export demand.

*Source: Chapter 1, Agents and their decisions (`sec:intro-economy`).*

## Markets and scale

Markets close the system. Population is exogenous and is the main scale
variable: it sets the size of the economy and of energy use and emissions.

There is no central bank in the current version, and therefore no monetary
policy rule, no nominal exchange rate and no policy interest rate. The
interest rate is a constant exogenous rate.

*Source: Chapter 1, Agents and their decisions; Time.*
