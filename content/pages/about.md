## Lineage

The model descends from GreenREFORM, the environmental-economic model used for
climate policy analysis by the Danish government, and through it from MAKRO,
DREAM’s macroeconomic model for the Danish Ministry of Finance. MAKRO is not
the EU model.

GreenREFORM-EU was developed for finance ministries, planning bureaus and
emissions agencies of EU Member States under the European Commission’s
Technical Support Instrument. The Danish KR Foundation funded GreenREFORM-EU
2.0 to refine the model, generalise the architecture, and produce this
documentation.

## How GreenREFORM was built in Denmark

Draft copy. The Danish parent was built as a shared framework across several
institutions, and that history is a better argument for the approach than any
claim the site could make about itself.

The idea came from Peter Birch Sørensen in 2016, while he chaired the Danish
Council on Climate Change. The tools then available did not let climate and
environmental considerations be built into the design of economic policy. He
wrote the grant application with Peter Stephensen, DREAM’s research director,
and it was awarded in 2017. In 2019 the Ministry of Finance joined and funded
a team of four model developers at DREAM.

What made it shared was governance rather than code. A project board chaired
by Sørensen met monthly for the first two years, each meeting followed by a
discussion with a larger group of the model’s potential users. Each
institution brought a requirement the model had to satisfy. The Ministry of
Finance needed the baseline macroeconomic and public finance paths to agree
with its existing forecasts. The Ministry of Climate, Energy and Utilities
needed the baseline energy balances and emissions to agree with theirs. The
Ministry of Taxation needed the tax bases and incentives described in full,
and a welfare measure consistent with the model. Statistics Denmark built the
input-output and energy data the model runs on, and now has permanent funding
to update it each year. The Danish Energy Agency supplies the costs and
adoption potential of future technologies.

It was not smooth. The paper records “persistent scepticism regarding the
development of a new model with overlapping features already available in
existing tools”, and repeated proposals to narrow the model’s scope. The
answer given was that the ambition is “not to supplant the work of the DEA,
but rather to establish a connection between the bottom-up approach favoured
by the DEA and the top-down macroeconomic approach favoured by the Ministry of
Finance and the Ministry of Taxation”. The first government use came in 2024,
when the expert group on agricultural emissions used GreenREFORM extensively
in its final report.

Do not read this across to GreenREFORM-EU as a property of the EU model.
Denmark has a decade and five institutions behind it; the TSI partners are one
institution per country. This is history and an argument, not a claim.

## Rights

GreenREFORM-EU is open source. DREAM holds the intellectual property in the
model. The licence text is to be confirmed by DREAM.

Results of the TSI 2024 project were licensed to the Commission under
conditions. Access described in the TSI final report is limited to
non-commercial, non-competitive use. Statistics Denmark holds rights to the
Danish benchmark data, which the open-source claim does not cover.

Do not name a licence or publish a licence page until DREAM confirms the
wording.
